# GP Brookneal OSB Plant — Brookneal/Gladys, VA

## Resolved location
**Roster coordinates were wrong.** The roster placed the facility at
37.050142, -78.94418 — that is the town center of Brookneal, VA, where
satellite imagery shows only small-town residential/commercial buildings,
no industrial mill.

Web research (Forest Products Locator, multiple business directories,
APA Wood) established that the actual GP Brookneal OSB mill is at
**11795 Brookneal Highway (US-501 South), Gladys, VA 24554** — Campbell
County, roughly 9 km north of the town of Brookneal. Listed coordinates
~37.1306, -79.0259 pointed at open farmland; probing southwest located
the mill. Locked center: **37.1232, -79.0320** — a large industrial
wood-products complex consistent with an OSB plant (process buildings,
steam plume, log yard, finished-goods warehouse).

GP built the Brookneal OSB mill in 1996; ~130 employees; produces
oriented strand board panels (Building Products division).

## Key views
- **z16/z17/z18 satellite** — Large industrial complex isolated in heavy
  woodland. Components: OSB process line and dryers (NW, with active
  steam/exhaust plume), a very large finished-goods warehouse (center/
  SE), employee parking lot (E), log yard with stacked logs and overhead
  log-handling cranes (W), settling pond (S), and product staging aprons.
- **Access road** — A single long access road winds ~1.2 km from US-501
  down to the plant; crosses an active rail spur on the way in.
- **Street View (Oct 2025) on the access road** — Looking toward the
  plant, the entrance shows a divided roadway (center median splitting
  in/out lanes), a booth-footprint structure on the left, flagpoles and
  signage — a controlled, staffed mill entrance.

## Gate / guard-shack / dock determinations
- **truckGate: true** — Divided entrance with median, flags and signage
  visible in Street View; controlled-access industrial mill entrance.
- **guardShack: true** — A small structure with a 1-vehicle footprint
  sits beside the divided entrance lanes — a staffed guard booth, the
  norm for an OSB mill that must check inbound logging trucks.
- **remoteGs: false** — Guard booth present, so not remote.
- **scale: true (medium confidence)** — OSB mills weigh inbound log
  trucks; a scale near the log-yard entrance is expected though not
  crisply resolved in overhead imagery. Flagged uncertain.
- **dockDoors: 0-10** — Few true dock doors. OSB is shipped on flatbed
  trailers, forklift-loaded from the finished-goods warehouse and from
  banded-unit staging aprons rather than through a dense dock bank.
- **shipRcvSeparate: true** — Inbound logs are received at the W log
  yard; finished OSB ships from the SE warehouse — physically separate.

## Yard zones and counts
- **Perimeter** — ~156 acres developed industrial footprint inside the
  cleared/fenced area (the broader wooded parcel is larger).
- **truckGate** — Box around the divided entrance ~150m in from the road.
- **dockApron** — Strip along the warehouse SW/S face.
- **staging** — Paved product-staging apron east of the warehouse.
- **dropYards** — None; no marked parked-trailer drop lot. Product is
  stored as banded OSB units, not trailers.
- dockDoorCount ~8, trailersVisible ~6, trailerParkingCapacity ~20,
  truckGateCount 1, buildingCount ~6, railServed **true** (rail spur
  with crossing gate confirmed in Street View).

## Final confidence
**Medium.** Location positively re-resolved and the plant clearly
identified. Satellite is winter/leaf-off and somewhat soft; the gate
structure is visible in Street View but no pano gets close enough to
confirm a barrier arm crisply. Dock-door count and scale are honest
estimates flagged as uncertain.
